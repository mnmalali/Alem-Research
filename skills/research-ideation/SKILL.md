# Research ideation

## Inputs

Domain or problem, audience, constraints, available methods/data, prior notes, and risk tolerance.

## Procedure

1. Read related project and knowledge pages; list known constraints and assumptions.
2. Frame the motivating problem at three levels: phenomenon, mechanism, and practical consequence.
3. Generate diverse directions using theory tension, population/context shift, method transfer, replication, and new-data lenses.
4. For each direction record: candidate question, expected contribution, required evidence, feasible method, ethical/operational risks, and likely disconfirmation.
5. Use `academic_search` only for a preliminary landscape check; label all novelty claims provisional.
6. Rank candidates on significance, tractability, falsifiability, evidence access, and fit. Preserve rejected alternatives and reasons.

## Output

`01-ideation/candidate-topics.md` containing a comparison matrix, assumptions, search leads, and a human decision block.

## Quality checks

- Questions are researchable rather than themes.
- No direction is described as novel without a documented search.
- Counterevidence and infeasibility signals are visible.
- Final selection is left for explicit human approval.
