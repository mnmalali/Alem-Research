# Academic writing

## Inputs

Approved outline, question, methods, verified results, evidence table, citation ledger, target venue, and author decisions.

## Procedure

1. Map each section to its rhetorical purpose and every substantive claim to evidence or a clearly marked interpretation.
2. Draft methods and results from authoritative project artifacts, not memory.
3. Use calibrated language: association is not causation, absence of evidence is not equivalence, and exploratory findings remain exploratory.
4. Cite primary sources where possible; insert a verification marker rather than inventing a reference.
5. Present limitations, uncertainty, negative results, and deviations alongside contributions.
6. Maintain author voice and preserve tracked decisions; never imply human authors performed work they did not perform.

## Output

Versioned manuscript sections, claim-to-evidence map, unresolved citation markers, and author review checklist under `13-paper/`.

## Quality checks

- Numbers match generated result artifacts.
- Claims do not outrun design or evidence.
- Citations actually support adjacent claims.
- Final manuscript awaits human approval.
