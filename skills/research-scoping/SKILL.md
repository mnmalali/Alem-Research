# Research scoping

## Inputs

Candidate topic, target contribution, audience, time/resources, known terminology, and existing evidence.

## Procedure

1. Define population/context, phenomenon or intervention, comparator, outcomes, and study/design boundaries where applicable.
2. Build a concept map with synonyms, adjacent terms, exclusions, and ambiguous usages.
3. State in-scope and out-of-scope claims, years, languages, source types, geographies, and methods.
4. Draft primary and secondary questions plus success and stop criteria.
5. Perform a small `academic_search` pilot and revise terms when retrieval is too broad or too narrow.
6. Record dependencies, risks, decisions, and assumptions requiring owner approval.

## Output

`01-ideation/scope.md` with a scope statement, concept table, eligibility rules, deliverables, and approval block.

## Quality checks

- Terms have operational meanings.
- Exclusions are justified, not convenient.
- Pilot search and resulting scope changes are recorded.
- Scope can be used unchanged by the literature-search skill.
